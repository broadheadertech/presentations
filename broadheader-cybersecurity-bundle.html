The premise

Breaches rarely start with a genius.

They start with something ordinary that nobody owned: a password reused across systems, a server left a version behind, an endpoint that trusts whatever the app sends it, an invoice email that looked right. Each has a different owner and a different fix — which is why one service never covers it.

Ordinary cause 01

A credential leaks, and the same one opens three more systems.

Ordinary cause 02

A public-facing service misses a patch cycle for four months.

Ordinary cause 03

An API checks the session but never checks whose record is being asked for.

Ordinary cause 04

A finance staffer clicks, types the password, and tells no one for a week.

Under the Data Privacy Act of 2012, an organisation that holds personal data is expected to appoint a Data Protection Officer, maintain security measures proportionate to the risk, and notify the National Privacy Commission and affected individuals within 72 hours of discovering a qualifying breach. Enterprise clients, funders, and app stores increasingly ask for the evidence before they sign.

Vulnerability assessment tells you what is weak. Penetration testing proves what an attacker could actually do with it. We do both, under written authorisation and an agreed scope.

Scope options

  • REST and GraphQL APIs, including authorisation logic

How it runs

03
Fix

Security Hardening

Most systems ship with defaults chosen for convenience. Hardening replaces those with a baseline chosen for exposure — then documents it so the next server is built the same way.

What we tighten
  • Access — key-based SSH, MFA everywhere, least-privilege roles, no shared accounts
  • Surface — close unused ports and services, segment networks, restrict admin planes
  • Patching — a real cadence with an emergency path for critical CVEs
  • Application edge — TLS configuration, security headers, WAF and rate limiting
  • Secrets — out of the repository, into a managed store, rotated
  • Database — encryption at rest, restricted network access, audited privileges
  • Visibility — centralised logs, alerting on the events that matter
  • Recovery — backups that are tested by restoring them, not by existing
What you receive
  • Hardening baseline document per system type
  • Before / after posture scorecard
  • Configuration scripts where automatable
  • Operations runbook for your team
  • Backup and restore test evidence
BenchmarksCIS Benchmarks for OS, container, and cloud baselines
Change controlStaged first, rolled out in a maintenance window, reversible
04
Drill

Phishing Simulation

Technical controls stop most mail. The rest depends on whether a person notices — and whether they feel safe reporting it. We test both, with the organisation's consent and without naming individuals.

Scenarios we run
  • Credential harvest via a cloned login page
  • Invoice and payment redirection to finance
  • Executive impersonation with an urgent request
  • Delivery, HR, or IT notice with an attachment
  • Optional SMS and voice pretexting
What we measure
  • Open rate
  • Click rate
  • Credential submission rate
  • Report rate — the number that matters
  • Median time to first report
  • Trend by department across quarters
What you receive
  • Campaign report with department-level breakdown
  • Immediate teachable moment page for anyone who clicks
  • Short training modules matched to the failure type
  • A reporting workflow your staff can actually use
  • Quarterly trend line for leadership

Run under a no-blame policy agreed with leadership and HR in advance. Submitted passwords are never stored — only the fact of submission. Results are reported as rates, not as a list of names.

Module Two

The Gateway

The payment rail.

Every peso the project handles — in and out — passes through one licensed rail. Not a wallet we built. A regulated payment gateway we integrate against, with the merchant experience built on top.

Payments module

A licensed gateway does the money.

Where a project needs to move real funds, we do not build a wallet. We integrate one that is already licensed to hold and move e-money, and we build the merchant experience on top of it.

Who they are

Licensed EMI

The gateway must hold a Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas licence — as an Electronic Money Issuer, an Operator of Payment Systems, or both. Unlicensed processors are not considered.

What it connects to

National rails

Reach into the national rails: InstaPay for real-time transfers, PESONet for batched settlement, and QR Ph, the national QR standard, for merchant acceptance.

What it moves

Cash in, cash out

Merchant funding and merchant payouts, settled to bank accounts and wallets. Philippine peso throughout.

What we build

The layer above

Merchant onboarding, the transaction dashboard, reconciliation, payout requests, fee logic, and the audit trail your finance team actually works from.

Division of responsibility: the gateway holds the funds, performs KYC on its account holders, and carries the regulatory obligation for custody and settlement. The platform layer above it — merchant onboarding, dashboard, reconciliation, payout logic — is built and operated on the project side, and earns its margin there. The provider is selected per project and named in the proposal.

Fund flow

Money in. Money out.

Two directions, one ledger. Every movement is recorded against the merchant account and reconciled daily.

Cash inFree or minimal to the payer; funds land in the merchant wallet
Cash outMerchant-initiated withdrawal; this is where fees are taken
SettlementPer the gateway's cut-off schedule; InstaPay real time, PESONet batched

Visibility

The dashboard.

Every peso that moves is visible, attributable, and exportable — a live ledger the merchant can work from without asking anyone for a report.

The ledger
  • Running balance, pending and cleared, at a glance
  • Transaction register: reference, channel, amount, status, timestamp
  • Withdrawal history with the fee shown per payout
  • Daily reconciliation against the gateway settlement files
  • Exceptions surfaced: unmatched, failed, and reversed transactions
Working with it
  • Filter by status, channel, and date range
  • Search by reference against the gateway's own records
  • Exports to CSV and XLSX for accounting
  • Role-based access: viewer, finance, admin
  • Full action log — who did what, and when

Every figure on the dashboard traces back to a gateway transaction reference, so any line can be reconciled against the provider's own records without a manual export.
